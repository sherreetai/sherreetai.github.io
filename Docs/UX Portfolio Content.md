# My O2 App Account Navigation

**Scope:** My O2 App\
**Role:** Product design, customer experience strategy\
**Outcome:** Halved product re-selection from 6 steps to 3 in the redesigned tariff-change and Bolt-On journeys.

## The Challenge

Customers faced unnecessary friction when switching between products and accounts. My role was to design a more intuitive, product-led navigation experience while working within the existing account and billing structure.

## 1. Built the missing system map

I brought together billing, backend engineering, frontline retail and eCare to understand how accounts, products and billing worked, then consolidated the findings into a shared IA and billing logic map.

The map captured different rules for finance agreements, companion devices and SIM Free Finance, rather than treating everything as one simple product hierarchy.

## 2. Shifted navigation from accounts to products

I redesigned the experience around the product customers were actively managing, while keeping billing information at account level. This brought the app closer to the product-led web experience and reduced the need to switch between accounts.

## 3. Simplified the product view

I replaced the carousel with a static product view and removed unnecessary hierarchy and headings, making the active product and key actions easier to find.

## 4. Removed unnecessary re-selection

I challenged journeys where customers selected a product, then had to select it again. Actions such as Buy Bolt-On could retain the selected product context and take customers directly to the relevant journey.

## Outcome

The redesigned multi-product journey reduced re-selection from 6 steps to 3 across tariff-change and Bolt-On flows. Carrying product context through the journey removed unnecessary steps and created a simpler, more consistent experience.

# Cross-sell and Upsell TV Product Cards

**Scope:** Virgin Media web\
**Role:** Product design, user research and experimentation\
**Outcome:** Redesigned TV product cards contributed to a reported 21% uplift in XSUS orders, with the winning variant reaching 95% statistical confidence.

## The Challenge

TV add-on cards created heavy scrolling and made products difficult to compare. My role was to use research to create a clearer product-card experience and structure the CRO test so we could understand which changes improved performance.

## 1. Simplified the product cards

I simplified the information hierarchy to make the product, value and action easier to understand at a glance. Research showed customers preferred larger product cards and clearer CTAs, even when this meant slightly more scrolling.

## 2. Used research to refine the design

I tested different approaches with 10 Virgin Media customers without TV. 7/10 preferred Variant Y, finding the CTA clearer and valuing the reassurance that products could easily be removed from their basket.

Wider research also showed customers wanted clearer product comparisons, including which channels were included rather than channel count alone.

## 3. Designed for real customer states

I co-designed the experience around different holding and eligibility states, including available, unavailable, selected and error scenarios.

I translated these into Gherkin acceptance criteria so design, engineering and QA shared the same expected behaviour.

## 4. Isolated the winning design change

I worked with CRO to test redesigned cards alone (V1) against redesigned cards with filter chips (V2). I kept the explored carousel out of the experiment to avoid introducing another variable.

## Outcome

The winning experience delivered a reported 21% uplift in XSUS orders. The filter-chip variant reached 95% statistical confidence, compared with 90% for the card-only variant, helping isolate the filters as the additional change driving the stronger result.

# VMO2 App Library Design System

**Scope:** Virgin Media, O2 and Priority apps\
**Role:** Design systems, component strategy, UX implementation\
**Outcome:** Made theme application ~93% faster, reducing it from 54+ seconds to under 4 seconds and saving an estimated 5–6 hours per designer each year.

## The Challenge

Teams were recreating similar components across brands, creating inconsistent states and design-to-development drift. My role was to help shape a scalable cross-brand system, then put it into practice by creating the My O2 App master file and implementing the new variable structure.

## 1. Audited the existing system

I helped audit repeated patterns and inconsistent component states across the apps, identifying where components could be consolidated into shared foundations.

## 2. Rebuilt components with Figma Variables

I helped rebuild components from atomic foundations using Figma Variables, replacing the slower Tokens Studio workflow with native theming and reducing reliance on third-party tooling.

## 3. Aligned design with development

I worked with engineering so component and variable logic reflected how the UI was built, helping reduce gaps between Figma and implementation.

## 4. Implemented the system in My O2 App

I created the My O2 App master file and was the first to implement the new Figma Variables into a live app design file. This proved the variable structure could work in practice and created a foundation for migrating components away from Tokens Studio.

## Outcome

Moving to Figma Variables reduced theme application from 54+ seconds to under 4 seconds, making it ~93% faster. Across 7,609 recorded component inserts, this was estimated to save 5–6 hours per designer each year, while creating a more scalable cross-brand foundation.

# VM Home Movers Journey

**Scope:** Virgin Media web\
**Role:** Product design, UX strategy and user research\
**Outcome:** Redesigned the Movers homepage around behavioural data and customer research, targeting the 55% drop-off before postcode entry.

## The Challenge

Only 45% of sessions progressed from the Movers homepage to postcode entry. Research also showed customers needed greater reassurance around availability, connectivity and what would happen during their move. My role was to understand the friction and use those findings to redesign the homepage and wider journey.

## 1. Analysed where customers were dropping out

I combined Contentsquare behavioural data, Tableau funnel data and 2026 user research to understand where customers were struggling and what they needed before starting their move.

## 2. Turned research into the homepage redesign

Research showed the homepage felt lengthy and static, so I simplified the content hierarchy around customers’ main questions and actions. I also introduced simple motion design to make key moments more engaging and break up the experience.

## 3. Looked beyond the homepage

I used the same evidence to review the wider serviceability and booking journey. Funnel analysis showed only 15% of serviceable sessions went on to start the web booking journey, highlighting another point of friction to address.

## 4. Tested and refined key decisions

I ran further research to validate parts of the redesigned experience, including how faster broadband could be communicated without distracting customers from their move. 94% of 89 participants chose one of the two prominent green notification treatments, which informed the direction for further refinement.

## Outcome

The work created an evidence-led redesign of the Movers homepage and wider journey, using behavioural data, customer research and testing to guide each design decision. The new direction targets the 55% drop-off before postcode entry, while creating a clearer path through serviceability and booking.
