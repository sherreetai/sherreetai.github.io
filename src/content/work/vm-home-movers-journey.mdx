<CaseSection number="02" title="Constraints">
  <p>The redesign had to balance customer needs with legal pricing requirements, legacy technology, different serviceability routes and fragmented journey ownership.</p>
  <p>Faster broadband could be surfaced, but upgrades couldn’t happen within the move itself so I needed to provide reassurance and communicate the opportunity without pulling customers away from completing their move.</p>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="03" title="Analysing where and why customers were dropping out" layout="three-column">
  <CaseItem title="Data used" placeholder media="/images/vm-home-movers/data-used.png">
    <p>Contentsquare behavioural data and Tableau funnel data.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <CaseItem title="Research" placeholder media="/images/vm-home-movers/research.png">
    <p>July 2026 user research into what customers needed before starting a move.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <CaseItem title="Key insight" placeholder media="/images/vm-home-movers/key-insight.png">
    <p>The homepage felt lengthy and static, while customers needed more reassurance before committing. Only 15% of serviceable sessions went on to start the web booking journey.</p>
  </CaseItem>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="04" title="Exploring beyond the existing journey">
  <p>I explored different ways to simplify the Movers homepage without removing information customers needed to feel confident progressing. This included reordering the information hierarchy, introducing a carousel and simple motion, prioritising FAQs, and testing different ways to communicate faster broadband availability.</p>
  <p>The options were shaped by the constraint that customers could explore upgrades but couldn’t complete one within the move journey, helping balance reassurance and commercial opportunity without adding another exit point.</p>
  <CaseQuote>Moving home is already complex. The experience didn’t need to add to it.</CaseQuote>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="05" title="The design" layout="two-column">
  <CaseItem title="Simpler hierarchy">
    <p>Organised the homepage around customers’ key questions and actions.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <CaseItem title="Simple motion design">
    <p>Made key moments more engaging and broke up the page.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <CasePlaceholder src="/images/vm-home-movers/homepage-redesign.png" wide spacing />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="06" title="Validating the evidence" layout="two-column">
  <CaseItem>
    <p>I tested three notification treatments to understand how clearly customers noticed and understood the faster-speed opportunity.</p>
    <p>94% preferred one of the two prominent treatments, helping refine the final direction around stronger visibility and clearer reassurance that upgrades happen after the move.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <CasePlaceholder src="/images/vm-home-movers/validation.png" spacing />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="07" title="What that evidence changed">
  <p>The research established a clearer direction for communicating faster broadband without interrupting the core move journey. The refined experience is now ready for CRO testing, where messaging such as “faster speeds” vs “4x faster” can be measured against progression and drop-off rather than stated preference alone.</p>
</CaseSection>
