<CaseSection number="02" title="Understanding existing behavioural data and user research" layout="two-column">
  <div class="space-y-8 md:self-center">
    <CaseItem title="Data used">
      <p>Contentsquare data showed an average 20% exit rate over 90 days on the TV journey, with heavy scrolling and difficulty comparing offers.</p>
    </CaseItem>
    <CaseItem title="Research and key insight">
      <p>User research showed customers think about channels before packages, so the cards needed to make channels easier to find and compare.</p>
    </CaseItem>
  </div>
  <div class="md:self-center">
    <CasePlaceholder src="/images/tv-product-cards/tv-journey-strip.webp" alt="The existing mobile Change my package page, split into four columns to show its full length.     <p class="mt-3 text-sm text-muted">The existing mobile TV journey, shown in four columns.</p>
  </div>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="03" title="Exploring and validating multiple interaction models">
  <p>I explored carousel, card and CTA variations to reduce scrolling and make TV products easier to browse and compare. I tested multiple concepts with customers, using discoverability, comparison and CTA clarity to evaluate them.</p>
  <p><strong>7 of 10 participants preferred Variant Y,</strong> helping narrow the direction before progressing to CRO experimentation. Customers preferred larger cards and clearer CTAs, even with slightly more scrolling. They also wanted to see which channels were included rather than only a channel count.</p>
  <CasePlaceholder src="/images/tv-product-cards/research-testing.png" spacing />
</CaseSection>
