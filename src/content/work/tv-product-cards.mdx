<CaseSection number="02" title="Finding the friction" layout="two-column">
  <div class="space-y-8">
    <CaseItem title="Data used">
      <p>Contentsquare data showed an average 20% exit rate over 90 days on the TV journey, with heavy scrolling and difficulty comparing offers.</p>
    </CaseItem>
    <CaseItem title="Research and key insight">
      <p>User research showed customers think about channels before packages, so the cards needed to make channels easier to find and compare.</p>
    </CaseItem>
  </div>
  <div slot="aside">

    <p class="mt-3 text-sm text-muted">The existing mobile TV journey, shown in four columns.</p>
  </div>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="03" title="Exploring options">
  <p>I explored carousel, card and button variations to reduce scrolling and make TV products easier to browse and compare. I tested multiple concepts with customers, using discoverability, comparison and button clarity to evaluate them.</p>
  <p><strong>7 of 10 participants preferred Variant Y, the card with a pill button,</strong> which narrowed the direction before CRO testing. Customers preferred larger cards and clearer buttons, even with slightly more scrolling. They also wanted to see which channels were included rather than only a channel count.</p>
  <CasePlaceholder src="/images/tv-product-cards/research-testing.webp" spacing />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="05" title="Outcome and next steps">
  <p>Cards with filter chips lifted orders by 21% at 95% confidence, beating cards alone. This version is now being rolled out. Next I'm testing the same card pattern on non-TV products such as broadband, SIM and WiFi Max.</p>
