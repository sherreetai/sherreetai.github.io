<CaseSection number="02" title="Constraints">
  <p>Eligibility, holdings and dependencies meant cards had to handle available, unavailable, selected and error states.</p>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="03" title="Understanding existing behavioural data and user research" layout="two-column">
  <CaseItem>
    <p><strong>Almost 40% of customers weren’t opening the product accordions,</strong> meaning many never saw the available products.</p>
    <p>Research showed customers thought about <strong>channels before packages,</strong> helping identify an opportunity to improve how TV options were structured and compared.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <CaseItem>
    <p><strong>Contentsquare data showed an average 20% exit rate over 90 days,</strong> while research highlighted heavy scrolling and difficulty comparing offers.</p>
    <p>Existing selection controls also created interaction limitations, while the cards <strong>needed to accommodate eligibility states, bundles and regulatory messaging</strong> such as the 14-day cancellation copy.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <CaseQuote>The challenge wasn’t simply redesigning the cards. It was helping customers discover, compare and select products more easily.</CaseQuote>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="04" title="Exploring and validating multiple interaction models">
  <p>I explored carousel, card and CTA variations to reduce scrolling and make TV products easier to browse and compare. I tested multiple concepts with customers, using discoverability, comparison and CTA clarity to evaluate them.</p>
  <p><strong>7 of 10 participants preferred Variant Y,</strong> helping narrow the direction before progressing to CRO experimentation. Customers preferred larger cards and clearer CTAs, even with slightly more scrolling, and wanted to see which channels were included rather than only a channel count.</p>
  <CasePlaceholder src="/images/tv-product-cards/research-testing.png" spacing />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="06" title="Outcome and next steps" layout="two-column">
  <CaseItem>
    <p>The filter-chip variant reached 95% statistical confidence, compared with 90% for the card-only variant, isolating filters as the additional change behind the stronger result.</p>
    <p>Next, the pattern will be tested across non-TV product cards to see whether the same hierarchy, comparison and selection principles scale across the wider experience.</p>
  </CaseItem>
