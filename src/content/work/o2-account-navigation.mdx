<CaseSection number="02" title="Constraints">
  <p>Design had to work within the existing account and billing structure.</p>
  <p>Different rules applied to finance agreements, companion devices and SIM Free Finance so there wasn’t a single product hierarchy.</p>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="03" title="Finding the friction" layout="two-column">
  <CaseItem title="Four inputs → one IA">
    <p><strong>Existing app and customer behaviour:</strong> Multiple accounts, multi-line switching, confusing account / product relationships. Customer testing also showed people going directly to Account looking for detailed usage, while extras were described as confusing.</p>
    <p><strong>Web and existing design patterns:</strong> Current O2 web billing, itemised billing and existing multi-MSISDN patterns.</p>
    <p><strong>Backend and billing rules:</strong> Billing-account level data, Netcracker rules, CCA / finance agreements, SIM only / standard plans and companion-device relationships.</p>
    <p><strong>API edge cases:</strong> Unavailable account names, closed accounts, incomplete adjustment-charge taxonomy and data-return limitations.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <CaseItem title="Research">
    <p>Discovery sessions with billing, backend engineering, frontline retail and product owners to understand how accounts, products and billing fit together.</p>
    <CaseItem title="Key insight">
      <p>The app had no shared map of its own logic so customers were being asked to navigate rules nobody had written down.</p>
    </CaseItem>
    <CasePlaceholder spacing />
  </CaseItem>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="04" title="Exploring options">
  <p>I explored fully product-led and flattened structures to reduce the emphasis on billing accounts. However, the billing investigation showed that products don’t consistently map 1:1 to accounts and billing itself is returned at account level.</p>
  <p>I therefore kept the account as the billing foundation while making product-level interactions more prominent. This gave customers a simpler experience without breaking the underlying billing logic.</p>
  <CaseQuote>The goal wasn’t to remove the account model. It was to stop exposing its complexity when customers didn’t need it.</CaseQuote>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="05" title="The design" layout="two-column">
  <CaseItem>
    <CaseItem title="Accounts to Products">
      <p>Redesigned around the product the customer is managing, with billing kept at account level. This matches the product-led web experiences and reduces account switching.</p>
    </CaseItem>
    <CaseItem title="Simpler product view">
      <p>Replaced the carousel with a static product view, removing unnecessary hierarchy and headings so the active product and key actions were easier to find.</p>
    </CaseItem>
    <CaseItem title="No re-selection">
      <p>Actions like Buy Bolt-On keep the selected product and go straight to the relevant journey.</p>
    </CaseItem>
  </CaseItem>
  <CasePlaceholder src="/images/o2/final-app.png" spacing />
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="06" title="Outcome and next steps">
  <p>The redesigned tariff-change and Bolt-On journeys reduced product re-selection from 6 steps to 3. Next, CRO testing will measure the impact on drop-off and identify any remaining friction.</p>
</CaseSection>
