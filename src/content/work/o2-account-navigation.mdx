brief: "Customers faced unnecessary friction when switching between products and accounts. I needed to design a more intuitive, product-led navigation experience that still worked within the existing account and billing structure."

<CaseSection number="02" title="Finding the friction" layout="two-column">
  <CaseItem title="Four inputs → one structure">
    <p><strong>App behaviour:</strong> Customers juggled multiple accounts and lines. In testing they went to Account looking for usage.</p>
    <p><strong>Web patterns:</strong> O2 web billing, itemised bills and multi-line patterns.</p>
    <p><strong>Billing rules:</strong> Account-level data, finance agreements and companion devices.</p>
    <p><strong>API edge cases:</strong> Closed accounts, missing account names and incomplete charge data.</p>
  </CaseItem>
  <div class="space-y-8">
    <CaseItem title="Research">
      <p>Discovery sessions with billing, engineering, retail and product owners mapped how accounts, products and billing fit together.</p>
    </CaseItem>
    <CaseItem title="Key insight">
      <p>The app had no shared map of its own logic, so customers were navigating rules nobody had written down.</p>
    </CaseItem>
  </div>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="03" title="Exploring options">
  <p>I explored fully product-led and flattened structures to reduce the emphasis on billing accounts. However, the billing investigation showed that products don’t consistently map 1:1 to accounts and billing itself is returned at account level.</p>
  <p>I therefore kept the account as the billing foundation while making product-level interactions more prominent. This gave customers a simpler experience without breaking the underlying billing logic.</p>

<CaseSection number="04" title="The design" layout="two-column">
  <div class="md:self-center">
  <CaseItem title="Accounts to Products">
    <p>Redesigned around the product the customer is managing, with billing kept at account level to match the product-led web experience and reduce account switching. A static product view replaced the carousel, removing unnecessary hierarchy and headings so the active product and key actions were easier to find.</p>
  </CaseItem>
