## Discovery

No single source of truth existed for the app's information architecture or billing hierarchy. Design decisions were being made against incomplete system understanding.

I brought together people from billing, backend engineering, frontline retail and web to map how the system actually behaved. This produced a consolidated IA and billing logic map that hadn't existed before, becoming a shared reference across the project team.

**The diagram below captures the four key areas mapped during discovery:**

**1 – Customer Account Structure:** How billing accounts are structured, what products sit within each, and how Apple Watches pair to their parent mobile phone number.

**2 – Direct Debit Setup Logic:** How the system assigns a new product to an existing or new billing account based on bank account state and product compatibility.

**3 – Apple Watch Bill Pairing Alignment Cases:** Two scenarios showing how billing account assignment changes depending on how many SIMs exist on the parent account.

**4 – Key Notes:** Edge cases and system constraints that informed design decisions, including legacy billing logic and SIM Free Finance behaviour.

## Design Decisions

### Navigation and product card testing

Redesigned to surface the most relevant product for day-to-day tasks while keeping billing visible at account level. A static panel replaced the carousel after meeting the 50% engagement threshold the carousel didn't reach, and a redundant heading was removed after testing showed it added noise without aiding navigation.

<Media src="/images/o2/card-testing.svg" alt="Product card testing" layout="panel" />

### Reducing Re-selection Friction

Previously, tapping "Buy Bolt-On" would surface a drawer prompting users to re-select their product before the bolt-on page loaded. The redesigned journey surfaces a panel of quick links directly from the account view, so tapping "Buy Bolt-On" loads the bolt-on page immediately with no re-selection required.

## Outcome

Re-selection steps were reduced from 6 to 3 for customers managing multiple products on tariff change and Bolt-On journeys. Alignment with the web experience reduced inconsistency for customers moving between app and browser.

The IA and billing logic map was adopted as a shared reference by the wider team, resolving ambiguity that had affected multiple work streams beyond this project.
