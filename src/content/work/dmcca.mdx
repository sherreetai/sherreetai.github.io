<CaseSection number="04" title="Exploring options" layout="two-column">
  <div class="space-y-8">
    <CaseItem title="Evidence from Acquisition">
      <p>Acquisition was solving the same DMCCA rules, so I worked with their designers. A comparable basket treatment tested neutral on conversion and went to 100% of traffic while the native build was made.</p>
      <p>I treated that as supporting evidence, not proof. Our journey still needed its own testing.</p>
    </CaseItem>
    <CaseItem title="More content isn’t simpler">
      <p>I looked at adding cancellation rights to product cards and condensing legal content at review. Some mandatory information couldn’t be hidden. Earlier tests also showed combined legal checkboxes performed worse.</p>
      <p>So I focused on hierarchy, timing and placement instead of cutting content.</p>
    </CaseItem>
  </div>

<CaseSection number="05" title="The design">
  <CaseItem title="The basket as the bridge">
    <p>The basket sits between choosing and committing. I redesigned it with a clearer order of product, price and contract details. Supporting legal information sits one tap away and anything only needed at commitment moves to review.</p>
  </CaseItem>

<CaseSection number="07" title="Outcome and next steps">
  <p>DMCCA became more than a legal update. It gave us a reason to rethink how 3 key purchase pages work together.</p>
  <p>The basket now bridges choosing and committing. The shared Figma file gives a base for build, research and CRO testing. I won’t claim the redesign lifted conversion. That still needs testing.</p>
