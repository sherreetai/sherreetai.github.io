## Design Decisions

### Customisation flow

Contentsquare session analysis surfaced early signals: Users were reading pricing breakdowns before dropping off, suggesting the 'thereafter' price was creating hesitation rather than the layout itself. Unintentional swipe-back behaviour from details to basket.

Using these findings as a starting point, I tested two prototype directions – Progressive reveal versus a per-page journey, to find which felt clearer and more intuitive. Testing confirmed users expected simplicity and transparency.

### Basket restructure

Iterations of a sticky mini-basket summary were tested and introduced to reflect monthly cost updates in real time as users customised their selection. This removed the need to scroll to track how changes affected their total.

**Mini-basket summary iterations:**

**1** – **Monthly and one-off totals** displayed separately, with a "View Summary" button giving customers a clear route to review their basket before committing.

**2** – The **primary action was elevated** to a "Checkout" button, with "View Summary" stepped down to a plain link to reduce visual competition between the two actions.

**3** – "Checkout" was refined to "Review" following feedback that checkout implied an immediate purchase. The **language change gave customers confidence** they could look over their basket before making a decision. "Main services" and "add-ons" labels were also introduced to clearly distinguish core plans from additional extras, each displayed with their contract length so customers understood exactly what they were committing to.

## Outcome

The progressive reveal direction was validated over the per-page journey for perceived simplicity and task confidence. The customisation redesign resolved the key friction points identified in testing and the basket restructure gave users a clearer view of what they were committing to.
